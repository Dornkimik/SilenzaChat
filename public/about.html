A NAME TO COME BACK TO

Join with an account

Keep your username across visits. All you need is a username and password.

Usernames: 3–24 letters, numbers or underscores. New passwords: 15–128 characters. Keep your password safe: there is no email recovery. Accounts keep your username, not your chat history or encryption keys. Signing in starts a separate identity and ends your current guest private chats and temporary-room memberships.

ROOM FOR EVERY KIND OF HELLO

Choose the chat room that fits

Public chat rooms

Talk with anyone in an open room. Public messages can be read by other visitors and the server. They are not end-to-end encrypted.

Encrypted private chat

Choose someone online to start a one-to-one chat. Your browser encrypts messages and attachments before sending them; the other person’s browser decrypts them.

Encrypted temporary rooms

Create an open or invite-only room for up to 20 members. Room messages and attachments are end-to-end encrypted for current members. Owners can share invite links, appoint moderators and set slow mode or disappearing messages. Rooms expire after up to 24 hours without activity or on server restart.

How to start an anonymous chat

  1. Open the chat. Choose a random guest alias or log in with a username and password. No email address needed.
  2. Pick a conversation. Enter a public room, select someone online for a private chat, or create a temporary room.
  3. Check before sharing. Public rooms are visible to others. For private chats, check the encryption status and compare verification codes through another trusted channel if you need to confirm a contact’s key.

What “anonymous” and “encrypted” mean here

As a guest, other visitors see a random alias. With an account, they see your chosen username. It does not make you untraceable: the server handles your IP address, and your host or network provider may keep logs. What you write, or show in a photo or video, can identify you.

Private chats and temporary rooms protect message contents with end-to-end encryption. The server can still see metadata such as aliases, room membership, message times, and encrypted data sizes. The website code delivered to your browser and your device also matter for security. The app has not had an independent security audit.

Recent chat history is held temporarily in server memory. A server restart clears it; private sessions expire after 24 hours offline. The separate Announcements section is an exception: admins publish updates there that remain until removed. SilenzaChat is for conversations, not permanent storage.
